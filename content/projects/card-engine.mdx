## Problem and outcome

Euchre needs clear ownership of cards, the pack, and player decisions so a
human seat and an AI seat can share one rules engine. The learning target is
C++ ADTs, inheritance, and polymorphism — not a GUI.

**Outcome:** a command-line simulator that deals from a pack file, runs trump
selection and trick-taking, and supports Human plus Simple AI players.

## Role and context

- **Course:** EECS 280, University of Michigan  
- **Spec:** [Project 3 — Euchre](https://eecs280staff.github.io/euchre/)  
- **My work:** `Card`, `Pack`, `Player` implementations and the `euchre` driver  
- Solo or partnership course rules; this write-up reflects my submission  

## Architecture

```
pack.in  →  Pack  →  deal  →  Player[4] (Human | Simple)
                              │
                              ├─ make trump
                              ├─ lead / play card
                              └─ score hands to game points
```

Players sit 0–3; teams are (0, 2) and (1, 3). The deck is the 24-card Euchre
pack (9–A in four suits).

## Technical decisions

**1. One Player interface for Human and Simple.** Virtual decisions for ordering
up, trump, and card play keep the driver free of `if (human)` branches.
Tradeoff: Simple AI strategy stays intentionally limited to course rules.

**2. Deterministic pack I/O for debugging.** Fixed `pack.in` and shuffle modes
make trump and play choices reproducible under the autograder system tests.

**3. Unit tests beside public compile checks.** Card / Pack / Player tests catch
ordering and left-bower edge cases before full-game runs.

## Validation

- Course unit tests and public compile checks  
- System tests against staff `.out.correct` traces (Simple-only and Human
  interactive paths)  
- Autograder checkpoint + full project submissions  

## Limitations

Console simulator for the EECS 280 rule set — not a networked multiplayer
client. Strategy is the course Simple AI, not a ranked bot.
