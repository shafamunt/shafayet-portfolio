## Problem and outcome

I needed a working sequential calculator on the course FPGA — not just a
combinational ALU — so an FSM could walk operands through add, subtract,
multiply, and divide and show a stable result on the board.

**Outcome:** a DE2-115 demo that computes four operations on 11-bit
two's-complement values from the switches and pushbuttons.

## Role and context

- **Course:** EECS 270 (digital logic), University of Michigan  
- **My work:** Verilog RTL for datapath and control, ModelSim checks, board bring-up  
- **Shared lab context:** same DE2-115 platform and project family as classmates
  (including public write-ups such as
  [Matthew Murad's portfolio](https://sites.google.com/umich.edu/matthewmurad/experiences/other-projects));
  the demo clip below matches that board and exercise

## Architecture

```
SW / KEY  →  control FSM  →  datapath (regs A, B, Q · ALU · muxes)
                              │
                              ▼
                         7-segment displays
```

![Conceptual datapath](/images/projects/fpga-calculator/datapath.png)

## Technical decisions and tradeoffs

**1. Strict datapath / control split.** Keeping the FSM out of the arithmetic
blocks made ModelSim traces readable when an op hung. Tradeoff: more wiring
between units than a monolithic module.

**2. Booth-style multiply instead of a naive array.** Fits the lab's bit-width
and lecture sequence. Tradeoff: longer sequence latency than a one-cycle toy
multiply.

**3. Board I/O as the product UI.** Switches and keys are the interface
recruiters can watch in a short clip — no host GUI. Tradeoff: awkward for
large demos; honest for a lab board.

## Validation

- ModelSim simulation of the FSM and arithmetic paths before bitfile bring-up  
- On-board checks with switch-entered operands (example classmates publish:
  `3 + (−4) = −1`)
