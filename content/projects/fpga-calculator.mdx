## Problem and outcome

Build a sequential calculator on the DE2-115 — not a one-cycle ALU. An FSM
walks operands through add, subtract, multiply, and divide and shows a stable
result on the HEX displays.

**Outcome:** `FourFuncCalc` datapath + controller for 11-bit two’s-complement
arithmetic, with ModelSim coverage and a board demo from switches and keys.

## Role and context

- **Course:** EECS 270 (digital logic), University of Michigan  
- **Spec:** [Project 7 — Sequential Calculator](https://www.eecs270.org/p7_sequential_calculator.html)  
- **My work:** complete `FourFuncCalc.v`, write `TestBench7.v`, wire `Project7.v`

## Architecture

```
SW[10:0] / KEY[*] / SW[17]
        │
        ▼
   FourFuncCalc ── controller (FSM) ── datapath (regs · mux · AddSub)
        │
        ├─ HEX7..4  operand (signed-magnitude entry)
        ├─ HEX3..0  result
        └─ LEDG[8]  overflow / divide-by-zero
```

![Datapath with shared AddSub](/images/projects/fpga-calculator/datapath.png)

## Technical decisions

**1. One shared AddSub for all four ops.** Matches the required Figure-4
datapath — multiply and divide reuse the same adder/subtractor through
multiplexing instead of extra arithmetic units. Tradeoff: longer sequences;
cleaner RTL review.

**2. Booth multiply embedded in the datapath.** Course sequential multiplication
algorithm, not a direct instantiate of the reference `BoothMul` module.
Overflow when the product will not fit in `W` bits.

**3. Divide on magnitudes; sign via XOR.** Repeated subtract until the
remainder goes negative (round toward zero); `sign(Q) = sign(dividend) XOR
sign(divisor)`. Divisor zero asserts overflow and holds until clear.

**4. External signed-magnitude, internal two’s complement.** Entry and display
stay SM via staff converters; arithmetic runs in TC.

## Validation

- ModelSim with state labels; parameterize `W` small first, then `W = 11`  
- Autograder checkpoint (ADD/SUB) and final (all four ops + overflow cases)  
- Board checks on DE2-115 — example sequence from the spec: enter, multiply,
  subtract, divide, clear

## Limitations

Course RTL lab — not productized IP. Division uses the project’s subtractive
algorithm, not a high-performance divider.
