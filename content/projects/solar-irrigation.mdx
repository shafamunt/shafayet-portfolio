## The problem

ENGR 100 asked for real hardware that moved and sensed — not a slide deck
about what we might build. Two threads: a solar-powered vehicle that had to
roll under a real panel mass, and an irrigation system that should not flood
dry soil on a timer.

## Solar vehicle

**Chassis as a packaging problem.** Drivetrain and panel placement moved every
few weeks. I owned the 3D-printed chassis — CAD in TinkerCAD / slicer stack in
PrusaSlicer — iterating wall thickness, mount points, and aero-ish shaping so
weight stayed honest while parts still fit.

**What mattered.** Print orientation, fastener access, and not cracking a
bracket the night before a demo. The "aerodynamics" line in the brief was
secondary to keeping the panel and wheels in one rigid story.

## Automated irrigation

**Sense before you water.** A soil moisture sensor feeds a microcontroller;
firmware decides when a valve or pump should run. The loop is simple on paper
and annoying in soil: thresholds drift, probes oxidize, and "wet" is not a
boolean until you calibrate it.

```
moisture probe  →  MCU threshold  →  valve / pump
     soil %            hysteresis         fluid only if dry
```

**Control.** Hysteresis keeps the actuator from chattering at the threshold.
The win condition is plants that get watered when they need it — not a
schedule that ignores rain week.

## Outcome

Two working prototypes and a lot of lessons about packaging, iteration, and
leading the physical side of a student team. Swap the illustrated cover for
