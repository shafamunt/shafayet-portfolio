## Problem and outcome

ENGR 100 asked for hardware that sensed and acted. I led a **solar-powered
irrigation prototype**: read soil moisture, decide wet vs dry on an Uno-class
MCU, and open a solenoid valve only when the probe said dry — on a rail fed by
a small panel and battery.

**Outcome:** a working lab demo with photos of the full loop (panel → power →
MCU → valve → soil cup).

## Role and context

- **Course:** ENGR 100, University of Michigan  
- **Role:** Hardware and prototyping lead on a student team  
- **What I personally owned:** sensor → MCU → valve path and thresholds;
  power bring-up (panel / battery / buck); 3D-printed mounts for boards and
  the reservoir  
- **Team context:** classmates shared mechanical, electrical, and presentation
  work; gallery photos are from our lab album  

## System / signal flow

```
solar panel + battery  →  buck converter  →  MCU rail
                                              │
moisture probe  ─────────────────────────────►│ threshold (+ hysteresis)
                                              ▼
                                         solenoid valve → tubing → soil
```

![Full prototype on the bench](/images/projects/solar-irrigation/cover.jpg)

## Technical decisions and tradeoffs

**1. Hysteresis on the moisture threshold.** Without it, the valve chatters at
the wet/dry boundary. Tradeoff: slightly slower response; much less wear and
noise in the demo.

**2. Characterize the panel before trusting the rail.** Dual-meter checks on
the panel kept us from blaming firmware for a brown-out. Tradeoff: more bench
time up front; fewer mystery resets later.

**3. 3D-printed mounts as packaging.** Custom stands for the bottle and boards
beat tape for a repeatable demo. Tradeoff: print iteration time; cleaner
handoffs on demo day.

## Validation

- Full loop exercised on the lab bench (soil cup, tubing, valve, solar rail)  
- Probe-driven on/off behavior checked qualitatively — I am not publishing
  numeric soil-% cutoffs without a logged dataset  
- Panel characterization with multimeters before demo  

## Limitations and next steps

- Course scope stopped at a working prototype — not a field-deployed product  
- Photos are lab-bench evidence, not long-term outdoor reliability data  
- Skills transfer into later PCB / embedded work (MESH, MRacing), not into a
  continued product line under this title
