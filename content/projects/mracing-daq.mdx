## Problem and current status

Lap times only help if the car can capture a pass at the start/finish line and
fold that event into telemetry — not as a stopwatch on the wall. I am working
on the sensing end of that path on [MRacing FSAE](https://www.mracing.engin.umich.edu/).

**Today:** a breadboard photosensor prototype and participation in design
reviews. **Not done yet:** signal conditioning, a board that owns the edge and
timestamp, and publishing onto the team's CAN / telemetry chain. I have not
implemented the full system.

## Role and context

- **Team:** MRacing FSAE (University of Michigan Formula SAE Electric)
- **Dates:** Aug 2025 – present
- **My focus:** vehicle software / instrumentation / powertrain support for
  lap-trigger sensing; Level 2 High Voltage (600V) certification for maintenance
  around the car's 600V system
- **What I personally own so far:** iterating the breadboard sensing idea and
  bringing questions into weekly reviews
- **What is shared team work:** harness strategy, bus conventions, and design
  reviews — I contribute; I do not claim sole authorship of CAN or telemetry

## Signal / data flow (target)

```
photosensor  →  conditioning  →  MCU
 beam break                         edge → timestamp
 at start/finish                          │
                                          ▼
                                   vehicle bus / telemetry
```

![Conceptual signal path from photosensor to vehicle bus](/images/projects/mracing-daq/signal-path.png)

Only the left side exists as a bench prototype today. Conditioning, MCU
latching, and bus publish are the planned next stages.

## Technical decisions and tradeoffs

**1. Start on the bench, not on the car.** A photosensor pass is cheap to try
with a breadboard. Putting an unfinished node on the car early would burn
harness time. Tradeoff: bench lighting is not paddock lighting, so false-trigger
behavior still has to be re-checked later on the real car.

**2. Treat the edge as the product, not the sensor part number.** The useful
artifact is a clean digital event the rest of the stack can trust. That pushes
work into thresholding / hysteresis / debounce before I obsess over a final MCU
choice. Tradeoff: slower path to a "finished" board, clearer interface for
reviews.

**3. Keep message IDs and firmware off this site.** Team IP stays internal.
Tradeoff: less public code for recruiters to clone; clearer honesty about what
is shareable.

## Validation

What I can say honestly today:

- Breadboard sensing has been exercised enough to support design-review
  discussion of the pass event
- I have not published scope captures, latency numbers, or on-car false-trigger
  rates here — those need measured evidence before they belong on this page

## Limitations and next steps

1. Design and build conditioning between the sensor and a digital input  
2. Move from breadboard to an Altium board revision that can latch time  
3. Agree how the event is represented on the vehicle bus, then integrate under
   review  

Level 2 High Voltage (600V) certification is a safety credential for working
around the EV — it is not an outcome of the DAQ itself.
