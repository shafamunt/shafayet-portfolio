## Why this exists

[MRacing](https://www.mracing.engin.umich.edu/) is Michigan's Formula SAE team —
almost forty years of building competitive formula cars, most recently a 10th
overall finish at Formula SAE Michigan and a push toward an even stronger 2027
season. Lap times only matter if the car can capture them reliably at the line
and fold them into the rest of the telemetry stack — not as a stopwatch on the
wall.

A beam break has to become a timestamp the team can trust later. That is the
system I'm building on: sensing, conditioning, latching, and publishing onto
the vehicle bus under weekly design review.

## Signal path

```
photosensor  →  conditioning  →  MCU
 beam break                         edge → timestamp
 at start/finish                          │
                                          ▼
                                   vehicle bus / telemetry
```

![Signal path from photosensor to vehicle bus](/images/projects/mracing-daq/signal-path.png)

**Photosensor.** A beam (or reflective) break at start/finish is the physical
event. The sensor has to fire cleanly when the car passes — not on every
flicker of ambient light in the paddock.

**Conditioning.** Raw photodiode or phototransistor swings are not digital
inputs. Thresholding, hysteresis, and edge shaping keep the MCU from seeing a
swarm of false edges when the car is near the line.

**MCU latch.** On a clean edge, firmware stamps time and holds a lap event the
rest of the stack can consume. The interesting bugs live here: debounce vs
missed laps, clock source, and how the event is represented before it hits the
bus.

**Vehicle bus.** The timestamp has to leave the node the same way everything
else on the car does — onto the team's CAN / telemetry chain — so post-run
tools see the same lap the track crew felt.

## What is real today

- Breadboard prototype with photosensors detecting a vehicle pass at the line
- Board work started in Altium for the node that will own the edge and stamp
- Weekly CAN bus and embedded control design reviews against the car's harness
- Level 2 High Voltage (600V) certification for safe work around the car's
  600V system

Team photos on this page come from the public [MRacing site](https://www.mracing.engin.umich.edu/)
the same way peer portfolios use team media for SPARK / FSAE context. Bench,
Altium, and scope slots are marked TODO until personal captures replace them.

## Still open

Conditioning between the photosensor and the digital input, the board revision
that owns the edge and the timestamp, and the exact shape of how that timestamp
is published onto the team's telemetry chain. This page does **not** list part
numbers, message IDs, or firmware source — that stays off the site while it is
team IP.

## What this taught me

Instrumentation on a race car is systems work: the sensor, the harness, the
MCU, and the people reading logs after a run all have to agree. Design reviews
force the diagram to match the car. High-voltage certification keeps the
firmware story honest about the environment the hardware lives in.

## Status

In progress with MRacing FSAE — iterating the sensing and acquisition path
against the car's real harness and review cadence for the current season.
